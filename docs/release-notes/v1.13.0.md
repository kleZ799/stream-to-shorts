You can see it working now — and if the app closes mid-run, you can carry on.

## Every stage shows what it is doing

A long video used to sit at 0% for ten minutes with nothing to look at, which
is indistinguishable from an app that has hung.

- **Fetching** now shows how much has arrived, **how fast it is coming**, and
  how long that leaves: *42% of 1.2GB at 8.4MB/s, 1m21s left*.
- **Transcribing** counts through the audio — *42% — 14m21s of 34m12s*. It is
  the longest stage, and until now it said nothing at all until it finished.
- **Ranking** and **Rendering** count their chunks and clips as before.
- Each stage **animates while it is the live one**, so there is always
  something moving.
- The header shows **time so far, and about how long is left** — worked out
  from how long this run has actually taken, and rounded honestly ("about 7
  min"), because a guess that claims to know the seconds is lying.

## If the app closes, pick the run back up

Close the app mid-run, lose power, or have something crash during a two-hour
VOD, and the next launch offers it back:

> **A run didn't finish.** *your stream* — it stopped while finding the best
> moments. Carrying on skips everything it already did. **Resume**

Resuming walks past everything already on disk — the download, the transcript,
and the ranking chunk by chunk — and starts at the first thing it had not
done. Tested by killing the app during ranking: it came back, resumed, and
finished in 98 seconds without re-downloading or re-transcribing anything.

## Downloads are quicker

The video now arrives in four pieces at once instead of one at a time, which
is a straight speed-up on most connections.

**StreamToShorts.exe** — about 220 MB, Windows, self-contained. Unsigned, so
SmartScreen will warn: **More info → Run anyway**.

**StreamToShorts-linux-x86_64** — about 278 MB, x86-64, self-contained, glibc
2.35 or newer. `chmod +x` it and run it. **Still untested on a desktop
distribution** — every published build is started by the release workflow and
asked for its interface before the release exists, but nobody has yet sat at
Fedora and made a Short with it. Take `StreamToShorts-linux-README.txt` too.

**StreamToShorts-macOS-arm64.zip** — about 165 MB, Apple Silicon only. **Still
a beta, and still has never been run on a Mac** — I do not own one, so it is
built and checked by GitHub's macOS runners and published without a human
opening it. If it does not start, that is a bug here:
`~/Movies/StreamToShorts/app.log` and
[an issue](https://github.com/kleZ799/stream-to-shorts/issues/new) are how it
gets fixed.

On v1.5.0 or later on Windows, or on any Linux build? The app will offer this
to you.
