Titles that name the right game, a webcam panel with only the webcam in it,
and a face crop that stops shaking.

## It looks at the clip before it names it

Until now every clip in a run was filed under one subject, worked out from the
video's title. A variety stream titled after The Finals that moved on to
Firewatch came back with Firewatch clips tagged `#thefinals` — because nothing
ever looked at the screen.

Now four frames from each clip are shown to the AI before a word is written.
It says what kind of video the clip is — gameplay, a podcast, a story told to
camera, a tutorial — and what is in it, and that decides the title, the tags
and the hashtags. Tags follow the kind of video too, so a podcast clip is no
longer tagged "gaming clips".

A game is only named when something confirms it: text on screen, a character
or place that belongs to it, the words in the clip, or the video's own title.
The AI is confidently wrong about look-alike games — it called a Fears to
Fathom clip Phasmophobia — so when it can only guess, the title says "this
horror game" instead. **What's in this clip**, at the top of Boost, shows what
each clip was filed under and why; type the real name there and hit Rewrite.
Each card in Your clips shows it too, so a wrong label is visible before you
upload rather than after.

## Five titles, ranked

Boost now shows five titles per clip, each on a different angle — the phrase
people search, the curiosity gap, the reaction, the exact detail, the stakes —
scored and ranked. Tap one to use it. Every suggested tag is a chip you can tap
in or out, with YouTube's 500-character count beside them, and each clip names
the search phrase it is written to rank for. Rewrite in Boost now rewrites just
that clip.

## The webcam panel is the webcam

The webcam-on-top layout used to crop a fixed size around the biggest face near
the corner. On real streams that meant gameplay and black bars inside the
webcam panel — and once, a baby's photo from inside the game instead of the
streamer.

It now samples the minutes around each clip, finds the face that keeps coming
back in the same place, finds the overlay's actual edges, and crops inside
them at the panel's real shape. If your camera fills the whole frame — a
podcast, a just-chatting segment — that clip gets the face-following crop
instead of being split in two.

## The face crop stops stuttering

The follow-my-face crop chased the face detector on every frame, and the
detector wobbles, so the picture shook. It now plans the whole move first: it
holds still while you stay near the middle, and when you move it eases after
you, starting and stopping smoothly. On a real face cam it changed direction 4
times in 20 seconds instead of 50.

## What this costs

One or two more AI requests per run, for the frames. On Gemini's free tier
that is well inside the daily allowance. Clips you made before this are looked
at the first time you Rewrite them, once.

---

**StreamToShorts.exe** — about 219 MB, Windows, self-contained. Unsigned, so
SmartScreen will warn: **More info → Run anyway**.

**StreamToShorts-linux-x86_64** — about 278 MB, x86-64, self-contained, glibc
2.35 or newer. `chmod +x` it and run it. **Still untested on a desktop
distribution** — every published build is started by the release workflow and
asked for its interface before the release exists, but nobody has yet sat at
Fedora and made a Short with it. Take `StreamToShorts-linux-README.txt` too.

**StreamToShorts-macOS-arm64.zip** — about 165 MB, Apple Silicon only. **Still
a beta, and still has never been run on a Mac** — I do not own one, so it is
built and checked by GitHub's macOS runners and published without a human
opening it. Everything in this release is Python and the interface, the same
on every platform; the one platform-specific piece, the path handed to ffmpeg
for the face crop, is written for both. If it does not start, that is a bug
here: `~/Movies/StreamToShorts/app.log` and
[an issue](https://github.com/kleZ799/stream-to-shorts/issues/new) are how it
gets fixed.

On v1.5.0 or later on Windows, or on any Linux build? The app will offer this
to you.
