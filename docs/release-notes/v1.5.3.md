A download that fails to assemble now says why, and usually recovers on its own.

## "Postprocessing: Conversion failed!"

Someone hit this and their log contained nothing else. That message is yt-dlp reporting that ffmpeg refused to combine the separate video and audio streams it had just downloaded — and the reason lives in ffmpeg's own output, which the app was discarding.

Three changes:

- **It retries.** If the merge fails, the download runs again without insisting on a particular container. Insisting is the usual cause, and the streams keep whatever container holds them natively. Nothing downstream cares — clips are cut with ffmpeg either way.
- **The retry is not silenced,** so ffmpeg's actual complaint reaches the log. If it fails a second time, the error names both attempts and says that a missing or broken ffmpeg is the remaining explanation.
- **A combination mp4 cannot hold now lands in mkv** rather than ending the run, and capped-quality downloads prefer H.264 with AAC, which needs no conversion at all.

**This is not claimed as a cure.** The obvious suspect — VP9 video with Opus audio forced into an mp4 — was tested against the ffmpeg this app ships and worked, so the original cause is still unknown. What changes is that the next occurrence produces something a person can act on instead of a dead end.

If you hit this, open **Show the log** and send what it says now — there will be a real reason in it.
